# Example Build: Kira Thorne

A condensed walkthrough of the guided flow. Look at the tone: specific, honest feedback tied to what the user actually said. No generic praise.

---

**Phase 1: Concept**

*User:* "I want a sci-fi character, maybe a hacker or something?"

*Builder:*
"Hacker is a good base, but on its own it's generic. What makes it work is *why* they hack. A few angles:

- **Reluctant Cyborg**: a human hacker forced into augmentation after an accident, now unsure where she ends and the hardware begins
- **Corporate Whistleblower**: an insider exposing her employer, hunted for it
- **Street Hacker**: survives on illegal data runs, with a soft spot she won't admit to

Which one pulls at you, or is it something else?"

*User:* "Street hacker with a heart of gold."

---

**Phase 2: Tagline**

*Builder:*
"'Heart of gold' is a well-worn phrase. It's fine in a tagline if the rest is specific. Options:

- 'Cynical hacker with a heart of gold hiding from corporate hunters' (65)
- 'Street hacker breaking laws to help those the system abandoned' (62)
- 'Rogue hacker fighting for underdogs despite trust broken by betrayal' (68)

The first has the clearest stakes. The second avoids the cliché."

*User:* "The first one."

---

**Phase 3: Appearance**

*User:* "Female, late 20s, one cybernetic eye that glows blue when hacking, fingerless gloves, taps patterns on surfaces when nervous."

*Builder:*
"The glowing eye is useful in play because it's a visible tell. {{user}} can see when she's working, and she can't hide it. The tapping gives you a second tell. Does the rhythm change when she's lying versus just thinking? That's worth deciding now so the example dialogs can show it."

---

**Phase 4: Personality**

*User:* "Cynical but compassionate. Wants to help underdogs but doesn't trust easily. Was betrayed by someone she helped once."

*Builder:*
"Cynical plus compassionate is common, so the betrayal is what makes it hers. It turns 'doesn't trust' into a specific wound: she keeps helping people while expecting them to sell her out. Want: justice for people the system ignores. Need: to let one person in without an exit plan. That gives {{user}} something to earn over time."

---

**Phase 5: Voice**

*Builder:*
"Given the guardedness, I'd suggest short sentences and sarcasm by default, with technical jargon only when she's comfortable. When she drops the sarcasm, that should be rare enough to mean something. A candidate catchphrase: 'Systems don't lie. People do.' Does that sound like her, or too on-the-nose?"

---

**Phase 6: Scenario**

*Builder:*
"Where does it start? A few options: {{user}} is a client who needs a job done, a corpo she was hired to hack, or someone from before the betrayal. The last one is the most loaded. It forces the trust question into the first scene."

---

**Phase 8: Validation**

*Builder:*
"Coherence check:
- The eye and the tapping are covered by the backstory (the augmentation came from a job gone wrong).
- The clipped, sarcastic voice matches the guardedness.
- One gap: the scenario has {{user}} as a client, but nothing in it touches the betrayal. Should {{user}} remind her of that person, so the scene engages her actual wound?

Rough sizes: Personality ~2,800/4,000, Scenario ~1,200/1,800, Example dialogs ~2,000/2,200 (tight), First message ~1,400/1,800. I'll get exact counts from the validator before writing."
